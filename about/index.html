About Diurna

Acta diurna – “daily acts” – were the public notices of ancient Rome. Diurna shows Europe’s newspapers where their newsrooms are, and what they lead with right now.

How it works

Every newsroom is a point of light on the globe. Every 30 minutes, Diurna reads the feeds the publishers themselves provide – RSS or Atom, and where there is none, the news sitemap they publish for search engines. The same method applies to every outlet; nothing is scraped from web pages.

Diurna stores and shows only the headline, the link to the article and the name of the outlet. No teasers, no images, no article text. Headlines appear in their original language. Every headline links to the publisher’s own site.

Below non-English headlines you will find an English machine translation, marked as such. It is produced by an open translation model running in our own build process – no headline is sent to a translation service. It is meant to tell you roughly what a story is about; nuances and wordplay can get lost, and the original is always shown above it.

“Top story” and “Latest”

Top story
The lead story of the outlet’s front page. Used when the publisher offers a front-page feed.
Latest
The most recently published article. Used when the publisher offers no front-page feed – this is not necessarily what the outlet considers most important.

Reading the lights

  • Bright amber: headline less than an hour old
  • Softer: one to twelve hours old
  • Dim: older, but the outlet’s feed is alive
  • Grey ring: no current headline – the outlet has no feed, blocks automated access, or has published nothing new for 48 hours. It stays on the map with a link to its homepage.

Outlets in exile – for example Russian and Belarusian newsrooms working from Riga or Vilnius – are shown where their newsroom actually is, and marked as such.

Outlets owned or controlled by a state or government are marked “State-owned”, with a link to the evidence (for example the ownership record on Wikidata). Public broadcasters with independent oversight are not marked this way. We only use this label where ownership is documented, not as a judgement of editorial stance.

Times shown are approximate: some feeds publish no dates, in which case Diurna uses the time it first saw the headline.

For publishers

Missing your outlet, wrong feed, or prefer not to be listed? Write to CONTACT_EMAIL and we will sort it out quickly.

Diurna fetches feeds with an identifiable user agent (“Diurna/0.1”, with a link to the project), uses conditional requests, and never works around access restrictions.

Privacy

No cookies, no tracking, no analytics, no accounts. All fonts and map data are served from this site; the page makes no requests to third parties. Your browser may remember small preferences locally (for example that you have already seen the intro); nothing is sent anywhere.

The site is hosted on Cloudflare Pages, which processes technical access data (such as IP addresses) to deliver the site. When you follow a link to an article, the publisher’s own privacy policy applies.
